Section II — Psychological Profile
Anchor — Object & Memory
A worn leather pouch containing three bone zarlar (dice), passed down from his grandfather — a professional gambler and part-time fal bakıcı (fortune-teller) in the back streets of Beyoğlu. Selim rolls them before every job. He does not gamble for money. He gambles for fate’s permission. If the dice come up wrong, he walks away. His grandfather’s last words: “The universe speaks in numbers, child. You only have to learn to count.”

Section V — Talents & Abilities
Recovery Talent (+1 Corruption)
Shadow Walker
For the duration of a scene, entirely imperceptible to mundane guards and cameras — they do not see you or register you on film. Attacking breaks the effect. Supernatural perception is not fooled. Selim mutters a dua (prayer) under his breath as he moves.
Acquisition Specialist Talent (+1 Corruption)
Ghost Entry
Bypass a single ordinary locked door, security system, or physical barrier without a roll. Against hardened or anomalous protection, spend additional Corruption to bypass instead of rolling. Leave no trace of entry.
Background Talent — Gambler (Passive)
+1 Deft Hands (effective Deft Hands 4)
Years reading cards, palming chips, and rolling dice in Istanbul’s back-room kahvehanes. His fingers move faster than the eye can follow. He still rolls the bones before every job — not for money, but for omens.
General Talent (Passive)
Paranormal Intuition
When entering a new room or location, roll Investigate (WIT) Difficulty 1. On success, the DA must tell you if a supernatural entity has been here in the last 24 hours. Selim calls it “feeling the djinn’s breath.” This roll cannot be pushed.

Section VII — Agent Biography
Before the Covenant — Who were you?
Born in the Tarlabaşı quarter of Istanbul, 1955. Mother was a seamstress; father disappeared when Selim was four — he believes the man was taken by djinn, and no evidence to the contrary has ever swayed him. Raised by his grandfather Orhan, a professional gambler and amateur fortune-teller who taught him three things: how to read a mark, how to palm a card, and how to listen when the universe whispers. By fourteen Selim was picking pockets in the Grand Bazaar. By twenty he was bypassing bank vault security for a crew operating out of a Beyoğlu kahvehane. He never stole from people who couldn’t afford it — a distinction that mattered to him deeply and to the law not at all. The Covenant found him in 1983 after he broke into a private collector’s vault in Geneva expecting jewels and found a Sumerian burial urn that whispered his grandfather’s name. He walked out with the urn, left everything else, and spent three days in a hotel room speaking to it. Wayfinder Counterintelligence picked him up at the Zurich train station. He asked if the urn was djinn-work. They said it was more complicated than that. He said: “It always is.” Seven years in Recovery. He still rolls the dice before every operation. He still walks away when the bones say no. The senior agents have stopped mocking him for it.
