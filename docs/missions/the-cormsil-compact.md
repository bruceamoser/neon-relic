# The Cormsil Compact

## The call

Covenant Wayfinder detected an occult signature beneath a small farming town in the Yorkshire Dales. The signature is ancient, pre-Roman, and recently awakened. Pattern analysis suggests a subterranean presence. An entity bound to a specific geographic location, previously dormant, now active.

## If the team does nothing

The twenty-five people seen escaping the library fire come home. At first they visit Eleanor Vane one at a time to forgive her. Then they arrive in groups and speak together. As Eleanor loses sleep, the same signs begin to appear beyond Cormsil. The church crypt holds the Society’s manual. The papers in Oxford and Sir Geoffrey Colne’s manuscript in London explain what the earlier negotiators promised.

## Behind the incident

A pre-Celtic presence in the earth beneath Cormsil. Named by the Saxons: "Dōmere". The Doomer, the Judge. Predates human settlement in Britain. It was already in the earth when the first Celts arrived. The Brigantes tribe encountered it when they dug a ritual well that intersected its resting place. They learned its nature: it responds to wrongful death. It cannot abide an unbalanced ledger. The Celts incorporated it into their justice system. The worst criminals were brought to the well and made to confess. If their crimes were severe enough, the Dōmere would claim them.

The Romans tried to destroy it during their conquest of the Brigantes (70s CE) and failed. The Dōmere cannot be killed, only contained. The Saxons built a church over the well, then a town around the church. The compact of 1882 was the first successful negotiation. All previous attempts at binding (including Sir Geoffrey Colne's 1642 effort) failed because they treated the spirit as an enemy rather than a party to an agreement.

Function: When someone dies within the Dōmere's radius by neglect, violence, or preventable mistake, the spirit may embody the deceased with a fragment of itself. The returned person appears alive and healthy. They retain memories, personality, relationships, but they are compelled to make the responsible person know what they did. This compulsion escalates: first visits, then statements of "forgiveness," then constant presence, then psychological breaking. The returned are not ghosts. They are vessels. They eat, sleep, speak, continue their lives. They don't understand what they're doing. The spirit acts through them without their knowledge.

Three weeks ago, the Cormsil library burned. Librarian Eleanor Vane fell asleep; a scented candle ignited a stack of books. Twenty-five people died, or should have. They were seen running into the woods behind the burning library. They emerged the next morning, unharmed, claiming no memory of the night. The town calls it a miracle.

But the 25 "survivors" are wrong. They blink too slowly (3–4 seconds between blinks). They're cold to the touch (~34°C). They finish each other's sentences. And every day, each of them visits Eleanor Vane to "forgive" her in voices that don't sound quite like their own. Eleanor hasn't slept since the fire.

The compact of 1882 (brokered by a mysterious organization known only as "The Society") was stored in the library's "History of Cormsil" section. It burned in the fire. The spirit is awake. It found negligence (a librarian who fell asleep), preventable death (25 people), and no acknowledgment. It is furious. It sees humanity as morally corrupt, it's been asleep for 105 years and woke to a world where people let candles burn down libraries. It wants to expand. It wants every wrongful death everywhere to produce a returned.

Covenant Wayfinder detected an ancient, waking occult signature beneath Cormsil. Agents arrive to a town where nothing is obviously wrong and everything is subtly wrong.

## People

**Eleanor Vane.** The Librarian: Accidental Cause of the Fire. Sleep. Just one night. She would trade anything. She doesn't know what the agents can do, but she'll help them however she can, if it means the visits stop.

**Tom Dwerryhouse.** Skeptical Farmer: Leader of the Doubters. Understand what happened to his wife. If the thing in his house is not Mary, he wants it gone. If there's a way to get Mary back, really back, he'll do anything.

**Reverend Paul Threlfall.** The Current Vicar: A Good Man Out of His Depth. Protect his parishioners. Understand what's happening in his church. Serve God by serving the truth, even if the truth is older than God.

**Mary Dwerryhouse (née Blake).** One of the Returned: Tom's Wife, Fragment of the Dōmere. Be Mary again. She doesn't know how. She would give up her borrowed life if someone told her that was the way.

**Margaret Hale.** The Volunteer: Returned Schoolteacher. Help the agents. End the Dōmere's hold on Cormsil. If that requires her to be the volunteer, she'll do it, not because she's brave, but because she's a teacher, and teachers solve problems.

**The Dōmere (The Spirit).** Antagonist as Condition: Ancient Subterranean Presence. Expand. Every wrongful death should produce a returned. The world is full of preventable deaths. The spirit sees them all now, it's awake, and its awareness is growing. It wants to fill the world with consequences.

## Places

**Cormsil Village (General).** A farming town of 400 people in the Yorkshire Dales. One pub (The Dōmere Arms. The pub has always been called that, no one knows why), one church (St. Cuthbert's, 12th century, built on older foundations), one library (burned), one school, scattered sheep farms. The village green has a medieval market cross. The atmosphere is tense. The town is divided between those calling the survival a miracle and those who are uneasy. The air feels heavier than it should.

**The Burned Library.** The remains of the Cormsil Public Library. A stone shell. The roof collapsed, the interior is ash and twisted shelving. The glass case that held the compact is visible in the reading room rubble: shattered, empty, but still labeled "Cormsil Compact, 1882: On Permanent Loan from the Town Council." The smell of burnt paper and melted plastic lingers. The site is cordoned off with police tape but not guarded. The fire investigator's report is filed at the town council office.

**St. Cuthbert's Church and Crypt.** A Norman church, 12th century, built on Saxon foundations. Stone walls, wooden pews, stained glass depicting local saints and agricultural scenes. The crypt is older, pre-Norman, possibly the original Saxon church floor. Accessed by stone stairs behind the vestry. The Society's manual is hidden behind a loose stone in the crypt's east wall, behind a wooden panel that hasn't been moved since 1882. The church vestry also holds parish records, including a fire-damaged partial copy of the compact.

**Eleanor Vane's Cottage.** A small stone cottage on the edge of Cormsil, surrounded by an overgrown garden: Eleanor hasn't tended it since the fire. The curtains are drawn. Inside: tidy, quiet, oppressive. Books everywhere: Eleanor was a librarian for 30 years. A clock ticks too loudly. Eleanor sits in her armchair in the front room. She doesn't move much. She's waiting for the next visit. The air in the cottage is stale and heavy. The windows haven't been opened in weeks.

**The Bodleian Library, Oxford.** The manuscript reading room of one of the world's oldest libraries. Wood-paneled, silent, smelling of old paper and leather. The vicar's papers are held here: Arthur Hailes, Vicar of Cormsil, 1876–1891: his personal correspondence, parish records, and a sealed letter to the Bishop of Ripon dated 1883. The letter describes the 1882 compact and The Society in cautious, uncomfortable terms. Also includes a reference to "the volume I have hidden, which I was instructed to return and did not."

**The British Library, London.** Manuscript collection. Sir Geoffrey Colne's "Of Certain Subterranean Presences in the County of York" (1642). A leather-bound folio. Colne was a 17th-century antiquarian who documented folklore, strange occurrences, and "things the Church prefers not to acknowledge." His manuscript describes the Dōmere, records a failed 1642 binding attempt, and includes, in the margins, in a different hand. An Old English riddle that encodes the spirit's true name. The manuscript is fragile; careful handling is required.

**The Standing Stone (Climax Site).** A weathered standing stone in a sheep field half a mile from Cormsil. Pre-Saxon, likely Brigantes. The farmer who owns the field (Tom Dwerryhouse) has noticed the stone is warm. It's never been warm before. The soil around it is darker. The grass within a 2-meter radius has died. This is the Dōmere's original resting place, where the Celts dug their ritual well, where the Saxons found it, where the church was built to contain it, where the compact was read aloud for 105 years. The well is still here, capped with a Saxon stone slab, overgrown, invisible unless you know where to look. The air around the stone hums at the edge of hearing. Birds avoid the radius.

## Possible outcomes

### Silence the Dōmere

Use the Society's manual + Colne's manuscript (British Library) to perform a ritual that forces the spirit into permanent dormancy. Requires three components: (1) the spirit's true name, "Dōmere", spoken at the standing stone, (2) soil from the pre-Saxon resting site (a handful dug from beneath the standing stone), and (3) a volunteer among the returned who chooses true death over borrowed existence. The ritual forces the spirit into dormancy, not death, but sleep. The Compact is replaced by a binding. The remaining 24 returned collapse, unconscious, and wake as themselves. The spirit's fragments withdrawn. They remember everything. This is its own kind of horror: 24 people who know they died, know they tormented Eleanor Vane, know they were vessels for something ancient and merciless.

### New Compact

Negotiate new terms with the Dōmere using the Society's manual as framework. The spirit is angry and distrustful after 105 years of being forgotten. Its demands will be harsh, possibly requiring a living human witness to remain in Cormsil permanently, or a more binding form of acknowledgment that cannot be destroyed by a single fire. The spirit understands negotiation; it accepted the 1882 compact because The Society treated it as a party to an agreement rather than a monster to be slain.

### Witness and Report

The agents fail to act. The Dōmere expands. The returned phenomenon begins appearing in neighboring towns. Every wrongful death in Yorkshire produces a returned within 48 hours. The Covenant files a containment failure. The Decay track advances by 1.

### Catastrophe

The spirit fully manifests. Every wrongful death in Yorkshire produces a returned within 24 hours. Eleanor Vane dies, not from violence, but from exhaustion; her body simply stops. The returned lose their anchor. They become directionless, then hostile, acting on the spirit's sense of injustice without human context. The Decay track advances by 3. Cormsil becomes a containment zone: 400 residents evacuated, 25 returned quarantined, the Dōmere's radius expanding.
