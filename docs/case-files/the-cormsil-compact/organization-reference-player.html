O1 — Library Fire Survivors (Number and Status Unconfirmed)

Status: ACTIVE — Under Investigation

Public Profile: An unknown number of Cormsil residents are reported to have been involved in the library fire approximately three weeks ago. The exact number of individuals, the nature of their involvement, and their current physical and psychological status is unconfirmed and under active investigation. Local accounts are contradictory — some describe the individuals as "survivors" of a tragic fire, others use language suggesting something more unusual occurred. These accounts may reflect local rumor, trauma response, or factual observation. The individuals reportedly reside in Cormsil and are going about their daily lives. Two individuals have been specifically identified: Mary Dwerryhouse (wife of Tom Dwerryhouse) and Margaret Hale (schoolteacher). Both are reported to have been present at the library during the fire. The survivors are reported to visit the former librarian (Eleanor Vane) at her cottage daily. The purpose and nature of these visits is unknown.

Known Activity: The reported survivors are residing in Cormsil and appear to be maintaining their pre-fire routines — farm work, teaching, daily life. Behavioral anomalies have been reported by local sources — specifics are subjective, unverified, and may reflect observer bias or local rumor. One survivor (Margaret Hale) has been observed watching other survivors with apparent analytical intent. The survivors' daily visits to Eleanor Vane are confirmed via surveillance; the content of these visits is unknown.

Assessment: The survivors are the central human element of this investigation. Their number, status, and connection to the subterranean anomaly are unknown. They are to be treated as civilians — potential witnesses or victims — until their status can be confirmed through direct interview and observation. They are not assessed as hostile. The daily visits to Eleanor Vane are a significant behavioral pattern requiring explanation. Source reliability: local accounts (contradictory, unverified), surveillance (confirmed for two individuals).

O2 — Skeptical Residents (Informal Faction)

Status: ACTIVE — Informal

Public Profile: A portion of Cormsil's approximately 400 residents are reported by local sources to be skeptical of the narrative circulating in the village regarding the library fire survivors. The exact number of skeptical residents and the specifics of the narrative they reject are under investigation. The most vocal skeptic identified is Tom Dwerryhouse, a farmer whose wife (Mary) is among the reported survivors. Dwerryhouse has reportedly been documenting his wife's behavior in a notebook — the content and duration of these notes is unknown. The skeptical faction is not organized — it consists of individuals who have independently noticed inconsistencies and are uneasy. The village is reportedly socially divided, with tension between those who accept the prevailing narrative and those who question it.

Known Activity: The skeptical residents are going about their daily lives. Tom Dwerryhouse has altered his routine to include daily visits to a standing stone on his property — the significance of this behavioral change is unknown. The skeptical faction has not taken any collective action. Individual skeptical residents may be more willing to speak candidly with investigators than those who have accepted the prevailing narrative.

Assessment: Potential source of unembellished local observations. Skeptical residents may provide more factual testimony than those committed to a particular narrative. Tom Dwerryhouse is the primary contact point for this group. The faction is not hostile and is not an organized entity — it is a loose collection of concerned individuals. Source reliability: local accounts (unconfirmed).

Further historical research is required. Do not assume the organization is hostile, cooperative, or still extant. The 1882 compact — if it can be located or reconstructed from historical records — may provide context for current events. Source reliability: single historical reference (unverified, fragmentary).

O4 — Eleanor Vane (Key Witness)

Status: ACTIVE — Priority Interview Subject

Public Profile: Eleanor Vane, age estimated early 60s, is the former librarian of the Cormsil Public Library. Civil records confirm residency in Cormsil. Employment at the library: estimated 20+ years. No criminal record. The subject was likely present at or near the library on the night of the fire — her proximity is probable given her role but unconfirmed. She has not left her cottage since the fire. Her physical condition shows signs of sustained sleep deprivation and psychological distress. She is reported to receive daily visitors — individuals described as library fire survivors — who arrive singly, stay briefly, and depart. The purpose and nature of these visits is unknown. The subject has not made any public statement about the fire.

Known Activity: The subject remains in her cottage with curtains drawn. She reportedly answers the door and is willing to speak with visitors. She is described by advance reconnaissance as "desperate for conversation with anyone not among the visitors." The daily visits are confirmed. The subject's testimony — if she can be interviewed — may be the most direct source of information about the fire, its circumstances, and the nature of the survivors.

Assessment: Highest priority witness. The subject's physical and psychological fragility require a careful approach — she is not a suspect and is assessed as cooperative but vulnerable. Her testimony should be obtained early in the investigation, before her condition potentially deteriorates further. The daily visit pattern is a significant anomaly that the subject may be able to explain. Source reliability: public records (high), surveillance (confirmed), local accounts (unconfirmed).
