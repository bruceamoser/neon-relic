Evidence Report — Unidentified Metallic Object

PRELIMINARY ASSESSMENT. Artifact analysis pending. Full classification requires physical examination.

DesignationUnidentified Iron Object — Provisional designation: "Crucifix" (Object of Interest: Prague)
Physical DescriptionAn iron crucifix of simple Latin cross form, approximately 14 cm × 7 cm. The iron is dark — surface patina consistent with significant age. No visible markings, inscriptions, or maker's marks have been reported. The object is described as notably heavy for its size. Preliminary reports indicate the iron is cold to the touch — consistently below ambient temperature regardless of environmental conditions. This thermal anomaly is unconfirmed and requires verification. The object's composition is of potential interest: preliminary metallurgical assessment (based on the forgery left in its place) suggests the original is not standard terrestrial iron. The forgery was produced using modern alloy — the original's material is described in fragmentary historical records as "iron from the sky."
Provenance (Preliminary)Origin: Central Europe, Bohemian Forest region (modern Šumava, Czech Republic). Historical records reference a pre-Christian religious site in the area associated with meteoric iron — a Slavic pagan community is recorded as having built a shrine at a meteorite impact site, believing the iron to be a fragment of divine armor. When Christianity arrived in the region (10th–11th centuries), the shrine was demolished and the iron was reportedly reforged into a crucifix. The crucifix entered the Martinice noble family in the 14th or 15th century through a marriage alliance. The family treated it as a protective heirloom. The crucifix was worn by Count Jaroslav Bořita of Martinice during the 1618 Defenestration of Prague — the Count survived a 17-meter fall from a castle window, an event historical records attribute to divine intervention. The last Countess Martinice donated the crucifix to Prague Castle in 1952 under pressure from the communist government. It was displayed as a minor historical curiosity. The object was stolen from Prague Castle approximately six weeks before case initiation, replaced with a professionally produced forgery. It is currently believed to be in the possession of a professional thief operating in Central Europe, possibly being brokered for private sale.

Anomalous Characteristics

Preliminary reports describe the object as consistently cold to the touch — below ambient temperature regardless of environmental conditions. This characteristic is anomalous for iron, which typically equilibrates with ambient temperature. The thermal anomaly has not been instrument-verified.

Historical records document a survival event inconsistent with normal human physical limits: a 17-meter fall from Prague Castle windows in 1618, survived by the individual wearing the crucifix. Three men were thrown from the window. Two died. The wearer of this object survived — and his survival was recorded as miraculous by contemporaries. Whether this survival was attributable to the object or to other factors (the fall was broken by a slope or debris pile — historical accounts differ) cannot be determined from historical records alone.

Reports from Vienna indicate a subject believed to be the current bearer of the object survived multiple point-blank gunshots without injury. These reports are unconfirmed — source is a single witness of unknown reliability. If confirmed, this would indicate the object provides some form of physical protection to the wearer. The mechanism, scope, and limitations of any such effect are unknown.

The object's reported material — meteoric iron — is uncommon but not unknown in historical artifacts. Meteoric iron was used in pre-industrial societies for objects of religious or ceremonial significance. The combination of anomalous material origin and anomalous physical effect, if both are confirmed, would be consistent with Class-2 artifact profiles in Covenant classification.

Additional anomalous characteristics may exist but have not been observed or reported. Physical examination is required for comprehensive assessment.

Handling Advisory

Recommendation: Do not handle the object with bare skin. Exercise standard precautionary protocols for unidentified materials of unknown provenance. The object should be contained in a sealed, lead-lined container if acquired.

The reported thermal anomaly — if confirmed — may indicate the object is not inert. Until physical examination can be conducted, the object should be treated as potentially hazardous. Do not wear the object. Do not attempt to test any protective or anomalous properties.

If historical accounts of the defenestration survival and the Vienna incident are accurate: the object may provide physical protection to the wearer. However, prolonged wear may carry psychological consequences. Anecdotal reports describe the current bearer as exhibiting increasingly erratic and reckless behavior — consistent with psychological deterioration observed in other Class-2 artifact proximity cases.

Advisory: This object has not been physically examined by qualified Covenant personnel. All characteristics described in this report are based on unverified preliminary reports, historical records, and single-source witness accounts. Treat with caution. Do not attempt independent testing. Await specialist analysis. Do not under any circumstances allow the object to be worn by team members.
