Preliminary Site Assessments — Macau

Seven sites identified for initial survey. Assessments compiled from public records, satellite imagery, and advance reconnaissance. Analysis pending.

L1 — Banco de Macau, Avenida de Almeida Ribeiro L1

PRELIMINARY ASSESSMENT
A Portuguese colonial-era bank on Macau's main commercial thoroughfare. The building dates to the early 20th century — stone façade, arched windows, polished brass fixtures visible from the street. Interior confirmed as marble flooring with high ceilings and ceiling fans. The bank offers private safety deposit services on a vault floor accessible behind the main teller counter. The vault system is reported to be time-locked — a pre-1975 mechanical model, standard for the region. The bank employs approximately 12 staff and maintains standard business hours. Public records indicate the institution has operated continuously since its founding. The vault floor is not accessible to the general public without an account. A warrant or court order would be required for access to rental records.
Accessibility: Day shifts only. Banking hours: 9 AM–5 PM, Monday through Friday. Closed weekends and public holidays. Vault access requires account holder presence or legal instrument.
Assessment: Site of potential interest. Financial records warrant further review. Source: public observation, satellite imagery.

L2 — Antiquities Dealership, Rua de São Paulo L2

PRELIMINARY ASSESSMENT
A narrow three-story commercial building on a cobblestone street near the ruins of St. Paul's. The ground floor operates as a licensed antiquities dealership — inventory visible through the front window includes porcelain, ivory carvings, brass instruments, and lacquered furniture. Public business registration lists the proprietor as João Rosário, Portuguese national, age 54. The building is wedged between a herbal medicine dispensary and a bakery. The upper floors appear to serve as the proprietor's residence. External observation notes: lights visible on the second floor during evening hours, third-floor windows darkened. The building shows signs of deferred maintenance — paint peeling on upper windows, slight structural lean. Business license is current. No criminal complaints on file with Macau authorities.
Accessibility: Day and Evening shifts. Shop open to the public during business hours. Upper floors: private residence, not publicly accessible. Night: premises typically occupied.
Assessment: Person of interest associated with this address. Business activity patterns warrant further investigation. Source: public records, business registry, external surveillance.

PRELIMINARY ASSESSMENT
A traditional Macanese tea house located on a narrow side street off the main commercial district. The establishment is divided into private alcoves by bamboo screens, with sliding paper doors opening onto a small interior courtyard containing a stone lantern. The business is licensed for food and beverage service and is open to the public during standard operating hours. The owner-operator is a Macau resident (identity unconfirmed — business registration under review). The premises contain multiple private rooms available for reservation. Recent observation: the proprietor has been noted burning incense on the premises outside of normal business practice. Significance unknown. The establishment's customer base appears to include both local regulars and occasional foreign visitors.
Accessibility: All shifts. Public access — enter as customers. Private rooms require reservation or purchase. Night hours: reduced operation, possible private functions.
Assessment: Potential meeting site. Pattern of private room usage warrants further observation. Source: public records, external surveillance.

The office is described in business registrations as a regional administrative hub. Building security: lobby checkpoint with visitor registration, keycard access for tenants, 24-hour security patrol. The building is accessible during standard business hours; after-hours access requires tenant sponsorship. The tenant corporation's public profile emphasizes wellness and alternative medicine. Recent corporate filings indicate expanded research activity in the Asia-Pacific region. No unusual security alerts or law enforcement activity reported at this address in the past 12 months. The corporation has no known criminal history in Hong Kong or Macau jurisdictions.
Availability: Day shifts only. Lobby security screens all visitors. After hours: keycard access required, 24-hour building security present.
Assessment: Corporate entity of potential relevance to current investigation. Financial activity and travel patterns of senior staff warrant review. Source: public records, business registry, building observation.

L5 — Rental Property, Coloane Island (Southern Coast) L5

PRELIMINARY ASSESSMENT
A small fishing cottage on the southern coast of Coloane Island, accessed via an unpaved road with minimal traffic. The structure is a two-room dwelling with corrugated metal roofing, showing salt-spray weathering consistent with coastal exposure. Satellite imagery confirms a vehicle is parked on the property — a rented sedan, registration traced to a Macau rental agency. The property's rental status: current tenant unknown, rental records not publicly accessible without legal instrument. The garden area is overgrown, suggesting limited maintenance. The nearest neighboring structure is approximately 500 meters distant. Police patrol presence in the area is assessed as minimal. The property's isolation and limited road access make it suitable for discreet occupancy.
Availability: All shifts. Remote location — approximately 1 shift travel from central Macau. No police presence. No immediate neighbors. Road access is unpaved and may be impassable in heavy rain.
Assessment: Location of potential operational interest. Isolation and limited oversight are notable. Occupant identity requires confirmation. Source: satellite imagery, rental agency records (partial), terrain analysis.

L6 — Camões Garden, Macau L6

PRELIMINARY ASSESSMENT
A public garden in central Macau, named after the 16th-century Portuguese poet Luís de Camões. The garden features banyan trees, mosaic-tiled benches in Portuguese style, stone chess tables, and a central grotto with a bronze bust of the poet. The garden is freely accessible to the public and is frequented by local residents, particularly during morning hours. The site is maintained by the Macau municipal government and has no restricted areas. Observation notes: the garden provides sightlines to multiple approach paths and has several exit points. An elderly individual — estimated mid-70s, consistent with a monastic appearance (grey robes, shaved head, walking stick) — has been observed in the garden during morning hours on multiple occasions over the past three weeks. Identity unconfirmed. The individual's routine and purpose are unknown.
Availability: All shifts. Public park — no access restrictions. Heaviest foot traffic during Morning and Day shifts. Minimal presence at Night.
Assessment: Public space of potential relevance. Individual observed warrants identification. Source: public observation, municipal records.

L7 — Abandoned Portuguese Fort, Coloane Island (Eastern Headland) L7

PRELIMINARY ASSESSMENT
A 17th-century Portuguese coastal fortification on Coloane Island's eastern headland. The structure is listed as a Class II historic site by Macau's Cultural Heritage office but is not actively maintained or staffed. Surviving features: partial stone walls (waist-to-chest height in most sections), several rusted cannon mounts, and a central courtyard approximately 15 meters across. The site is accessible via a single unpaved road. The location is exposed — on three sides, the headland drops into the South China Sea. The fort sees minimal visitation due to its remote location and lack of tourist amenities. Recent satellite imagery indicates possible recent activity in the courtyard area — surface disruption inconsistent with natural weathering. The nature and timing of this activity is under analysis. The site's isolation and limited access make it suitable for discreet use.
Availability: All shifts. Accessible via ferry to Coloane, then jeep or foot. No security, no staff, no lighting. The access road is unpaved and may be impassable in heavy weather. Exercise caution near cliff edges — no safety barriers present.
Assessment: Remote site showing possible recent human activity. Strategic isolation warrants investigation. Source: satellite imagery, heritage registry, terrain analysis.
