[#chapter-equipment]
= 1980s Equipment and Analog Gear

This chapter covers the physical tools available to Covenant agents: investigative gear, weapons, armor, vehicles, and consumable supplies. It also describes the Requisition system (how agents acquire equipment through their Clearance Level), the Resource Die system (tracking consumable supplies), Encumbrance (what agents can carry), Vehicle Rules (chases, damage, and repair), and the Crafting and Repair rules (maintaining and improvising gear in the field).

Repairs and gear degradation connect to the core system described in xref:chapters/07-health-damage-armor.adoc#chapter-health[Ch 7 — Health, Damage & Armor] and xref:chapters/03-core-resolution.adoc#chapter-core-resolution[Ch 3 — Core Resolution]. Refer to xref:chapters/05-combat.adoc#chapter-combat[Ch 5 — Combat] for the Ammo Die and vehicle chase rules that run during active scenes.

---

Navigating the neon-lit streets and abandoned industrial sectors of the 1980s requires specialized equipment. Items provide a *Gear Bonus* (extra black d6 dice) to specific skill rolls. Pushing a roll can cause the item to break, temporarily reducing its bonus.

To reflect the covert nature of the Covenant, characters do not use personal cash. Instead, they utilize their *Clearance Level (CL)* during the Equipping Phase to requisition supplies from Stack. Items are categorized by their required CL.

== Consumables and Resource Dice

Consumable supplies — ammunition, medical gear, batteries, and rations — are tracked with *Resource Dice*. A Resource Die is a single die (d4, d6, d8, d10, or d12) that represents how much of a supply remains. Larger dice mean more supply. When supply runs low, the die steps down.

=== How Resource Dice Work

*When to roll:* After each scene in which a consumable is meaningfully used — not for every bullet fired, but for every fight involving firearms, every Heal action using a medical kit, every device run on battery power for an extended period.

*The roll:* Roll the current Resource Die:

* *Result 1–2:* Supply is diminishing. *Step the die down one size* (d12→d10→d8→d6→d4).
* *Result 3+:* Supply is adequate. No change.

*At d4, a result of 1–2:* The supply is *depleted*. Remove the resource from the character's gear. The activity is completed (if mid-scene), but no more uses remain.

[%header,cols="^1,3"]
|===
| Die | Supply Level

| d12 | Fully stocked — fresh from Stack.
| d10 | Well supplied — no concerns yet.
| d8 | Adequate — a couple of heavy uses left.
| d6 | Running low — ration carefully.
| d4 | Critical — final uses. Do not count on it.
| Depleted | Gone. Cannot be used.
|===

=== Restocking

Supplies can be restocked:

* During the *Equipping Phase* at the start of each Case File: any supply can be restocked to its starting die size at no cost (subject to CL restrictions).
* During a *Case File*, at a credible source: a hospital supply room, a hardware store, an abandoned military cache. The DA determines availability; resupply takes time and may require a Tech or Deft Hands roll (Difficulty 1–2) to access without authorization.
* Cell *Underground Field Medic* (Personnel): once per Case File, restocks any medical supply to d8 automatically.

=== Scarcity in the 1980s Field

In the 1980s, certain supplies are significantly harder to replace in the field:

* *Specialized batteries* (lithium, proprietary formats for early specialist equipment) may not be available at the nearest grocery store — a Tech roll (Difficulty 2) to substitute or a Contact call to track one down.
* *Trauma medical supplies* require pharmacy or hospital access — risky in circumstances where the team is operating off the books.
* *Rifle ammunition* requires a gun shop or military supply — not every rural area has one, and a city purchase is traceable.

The DA should use scarcity as an atmospheric element, not a mechanical trap. Resource depletion should create pressure and decision points, not dead ends.

== Encumbrance

Every agent can carry a limited amount of gear before it slows them down. Encumbrance (*Enc.*) is listed for each item in the equipment tables above.

=== Encumbrance Scale

[%header,cols="^1,3"]
|===
| Enc. | Examples

| 1/2 | Small concealable items: a pocket recorder, lockpick kit, walkie-talkie.
| 1 | Standard field gear: first aid kit, camera, crowbar, pistol.
| 2 | Heavy or bulky gear: trauma surgical kit, thermal camera, shotgun.
| 3+ | Oversized or special items: heavy weapons, large artifact containment cases.
|===

=== Carry Capacity

An agent's carry capacity is *Strength × 2* Enc. points.

Wearing armor adds its *Enc* value to your total Enc (Concealed Kevlar = +1; Tactical Riot Armor = +3).

=== Encumbered

If your total Enc. exceeds your carry capacity, you are *Encumbered*:

* −1 die on all Strength and Agility rolls.
* Moving to a new zone costs a *Slow Action* instead of a Fast Action.

You remain Encumbered until your load drops back within capacity — drop gear, stash it in the car, or pass it to a teammate.

=== Overloaded

If your Enc. exceeds *Strength × 3*, you are *Overloaded*:

* You *cannot move to a different zone* at all.
* At the DA's discretion, you cannot take Fast Actions for movement.
* You must drop excess gear before you can move.

> *Overloaded* is a hard stop. You simply cannot move until weight is reduced.

=== Clearance Levels (CL) and Requisition

Every item lists a *Clearance Level (CL)* requirement. CL governs *equipment requisition only* — it does not control information access or institutional authority (those are governed by Covenant Standing; see xref:chapters/12-headquarters.adoc#covenant-standing[Covenant Standing]).

Agents requisition gear during the *Equipping Phase* between Case Files through their Division's Stack contact. Your character's current CL determines which items you can request:

[%header,cols="^1,3"]
|===
| CL | Who Can Access

| 1 | All agents — basic field supplies.
| 2 | Agents at Division default (Recovery) or with one completed Case File.
| 3 | Agents at Division default (Wayfinder, Keep) or with field commendations — this is the starting CL for Wayfinder and Keep agents.
| 4 | Senior agents and Warden rank — restricted hardware and experimental tech.
| 5 | Maximum clearance — prototype equipment and Covenant legacy assets.
|===

Starting CL is set by Division and Age Group (see xref:chapters/02-character-creation.adoc#chapter-character-creation[Character Creation]). CL advancement is handled through the *Requisition Authority* general talent (see xref:chapters/13-advancement.adoc#chapter-advancement[Advancement]).

[[requisitioning-gear]]
=== Requisitioning Gear

The Equipping Phase is a formal procedure conducted between Case Files. Every agent has a *CL Pool* equal to their personal Clearance Level. This pool represents the total value of equipment the Covenant authorizes for a single mission — it is not a credit balance or currency, but a trust allocation from Stack.

==== CL-Pool Point Buy (Rule 1: Automatic Requisition)

An agent may requisition any combination of items whose CL values sum to *less than or equal to* their CL Pool. No roll is required for items within budget.

*Example:* An agent with CL 4 could requisition:
* Four CL 1 items (4 × 1 = 4)
* Two CL 2 items (2 × 2 = 4)
* One CL 3 item and one CL 1 item (3 + 1 = 4)
* A single CL 4 item

Consumable supplies (rations, bandages, painkillers, flares, chalk) follow the same CL-pool rules as permanent gear — each consumable item has a CL value listed in its stat block and counts against the agent's CL Pool like any other requisition. The Resource Die system (see <<resource-dice>>) tracks usage after requisition, not during the Equipping Phase.

Unspent CL Pool does not carry over between Case Files. Use it or lose it.

==== Above-CL Requisition Roll (Rule 2: Pushing Your Luck)

An agent may request *one item* that exceeds their CL Pool — but only the item that pushes the total over budget. To do so, roll *1d6*:

* *6:* Request approved. The item is issued.
* *1–5:* Request denied. The item is not available for this Case File. The agent does not lose their CL Pool allocation — they may still requisition items within budget.

Only one above-CL roll is permitted per Equipping Phase — choose your over-budget item carefully.

Stack agents gain a departmental advantage: their requisition roll succeeds on a *5 or 6* instead of only a 6, reflecting their logistics connections and Quartermaster relationships.

The *Requisition Expert* talent (Stack) and *Black-Market Armory* HQ upgrade (see xref:chapters/12-headquarters.adoc#chapter-headquarters[Headquarters]) can modify or bypass this roll — see their respective entries for full rules.

==== Equipment Sharing (Rule 3: Checked Out vs. Held)

Agents may share equipment during a Case File. The agent who *checked out* the item from Stack remains the official custodian and is responsible for its return. The agent *holding* the item carries and uses it in the field.

To transfer custody, both agents must be present at HQ or in a secure field location. The transfer is noted on both character sheets. If an item is lost, damaged, or destroyed, the *checking-out agent* is held accountable — Stack tracks equipment by requisitioner, not by current holder.

If an agent leaves the cell (death, retirement, reassignment), all items they checked out must be returned or reassigned before the next Equipping Phase. Unreturned items count against the cell's next Standing review.

==== Equipment Return (Rule 4: End of Case File)

All requisitioned items must be returned to Stack during the *Aftermath Phase* at the conclusion of a Case File. Return is automatic — no roll required — but the following conditions apply:

* *Consumables:* Any unused consumable items are returned to Stack inventory. Used consumables are logged as expended.
* *Damaged gear:* Items that suffered gear degradation during the Case File (Gear Dice stepped down) are returned in their degraded state. Stack repairs them between cases at no cost to the agent, but the repair may not be complete before the next Equipping Phase if the cell is on a tight turnaround.
* *Lost or destroyed items:* The checking-out agent must file a Loss Report. A single lost item has no mechanical penalty. Multiple losses in one Case File, or a pattern of carelessness across cases, may trigger a Standing review or restrict future requisition privileges at the DA's discretion.
* *Signature items:* Division Items (Verdant Codex, Verdant Satchel, Warden's Bracer) are permanently assigned and are *not* returned between cases. Starting kit items purchased with character creation resources are similarly permanent.

==== Owned vs. Requisitioned Items

Some items are permanently *owned* by the agent and are never returned:

* Division Items (Step 7 of character creation)
* Starting kit items (Step 8 of character creation)
* Items acquired through the *Personal Phase* or narrative rewards
* Signature weapons or custom gear crafted during downtime (see <<improvised-items>>)

All other items are *requisitioned* — issued by Stack for a single Case File and returned at its conclusion. An agent may requisition the same item for multiple consecutive Case Files if it remains within their CL Pool each time.

== Vehicle Rules

Vehicles are field assets — tools to get there, get away, and occasionally get between a colleague and a bullet. They are not invulnerable, and the 1980s makes every breakdown feel earned.

=== Vehicle Stat Block Format

Each vehicle has five stats:

* *Speed:* A relative rating — Slow, Medium, Fast, or Very Fast. Sets the Difficulty modifier on chase pursuit rolls (see Chase Rules below).
* *Armor Rating (AR):* Damage reduction against attacks targeting the vehicle directly (ramming, gunfire). Characters inside an armored vehicle use the vehicle's AR *instead of* their worn armor while they remain inside — the vehicle's protection replaces, not adds to, personal armor (consistent with xref:chapters/05-combat.adoc#chapter-combat[Ch 5 cover rules]). Wearing armor outside the vehicle provides the worn AR as normal.
* *Reliability:* The vehicle's maximum Wear before breakdown. Each instance of vehicle damage or a pushed vehicle roll adds 1 Wear. *At half Reliability (round down):* −1 die on all vehicle rolls. *At full Reliability Wear:* the vehicle stops. _Example: a vehicle with Reliability 4 takes a −1 die penalty at 2 Wear and stops at 4 Wear._
* *Handling:* A bonus or penalty applied to all pursuit and evasion rolls (Wits or Agility depending on maneuver type). Represents how nimble the vehicle is.
* *Capacity:* Number of occupants (passengers + driver) and approximate cargo Enc.

[[crafting-and-repair]]
== Crafting and Repair

Gear breaks. In the 1980s, that means hunting down parts, improvising solutions,
and relying on whoever in your cell knows which end of a soldering iron is hot.
Stack Division agents are the backbone of field readiness — but any experienced
agent can manage a field fix if the situation demands it.

> *Terminology clarification:* This chapter uses "Gear Die" (black d6) and "Resource Die" (the stepped d4–d12 supply tracker) as distinct terms. *Gear Dice* are the black d6 dice in any skill roll pool — rolling a 1 on any Gear Die may degrade the item. *Resource Dice* are the consumable supply trackers (stepping down from d12 → d4 → Depleted). These are different systems.

---

=== Gear Degradation

Every item with a *Gear Bonus* risks degradation when any Gear Die in the pool
rolls a *1* on the initial roll (not on a push — Gear Dice are never rerolled when pushing). The item's Gear Bonus is permanently reduced by 1 until repaired. An item reduced to Gear Bonus 0 is *Broken* and cannot be used.

[NOTE]
====
This degradation system is identical to the one in xref:chapters/07-health-damage-armor.adoc#chapter-health[Ch 7 — Health, Damage & Armor] and xref:chapters/03-core-resolution.adoc#chapter-core-resolution[Ch 3 — Core Resolution]. Ch 7 is the canonical source; this chapter repeats the relevant rules for equipment-specific context only.
====

This works consistently across all item types:

* *Weapons and tools:* A 1 on any Gear Die reduces the Gear Bonus by 1 (fewer dice on the next roll).
* *Armor:* A 1 on any armor-save Gear Die reduces the Armor Rating by 1.
* *Improvised items:* Begin with Gear Bonus 1 (one black d6). A 1 breaks them immediately — roll on the Improvised Item Failure table below instead of reducing Gear Bonus.

Gear Dice are never rerolled on a push. See xref:chapters/03-core-resolution.adoc#_gear_degradation[Core Resolution → Gear Degradation] for the full rule.

> Stack Division Talent *Jury-Rig* can restore a Broken item mid-scene at the cost
> of +1 Corruption. Formal repair rules below handle everything else.

---

=== Repair Rules

Repairing gear requires **time**, **parts**, and a **Tech** roll.

[[repair-roll]]
==== The Repair Roll

[cols="1,3",frame=sides,grid=rows]
|===
| *Attribute* | Wits (WIT)
| *Skill*     | Tech _(Stack signature skill)_
| *Base Difficulty* | Varies by condition (see table below)
| *Time Required* | Varies (see table below)
| *Tools Required* | Basic toolkit (most repairs); specialized kit for electronics/artifacts
|===

[%header,cols="2,1,2,3"]
|===
| Condition | Gear Bonus | Repair Diff | Time Required

| *Degraded* (Gear Bonus below max) | Below max (≥1) | 1 | Quick Repair: 15 minutes (1 scene)
| *Broken* (Gear Bonus 0, non-electronic) | 0 | 2 | Full Repair: 1 Shift (~4 hours)
| *Broken* (Gear Bonus 0, electronic/mechanical) | 0 | 3 | Full Repair: 1 Shift; requires Power Tools
| *Artifact-Adjacent Damage* | — | 4 | Requires Occult Lab or Wayfinder consult
|===

[NOTE]
====
*Vehicle repair* uses a separate system — see xref:chapters/appendix-combat.adoc#vehicle-damage[Appendix: Advanced Combat → Vehicle Damage] (Tech roll Difficulty 2, 30 minutes per 1 Wear cleared). Do not use this table for vehicle Wear.
====

**Success:** Each success restores Gear Bonus by 1 (up to the item's maximum).
A single Quick Repair roll can restore a Degraded item but not a Broken one —
a Broken item requires a Full Repair before it can be used again.

**Failure:** The repair attempt fails but does not make things worse.
**Complication (Pushed roll only):** A pushed Repair roll that fails delivers a
Complication roll on the <<gear-breakdown,Breakdown Table>>.

[[repair-parts]]
==== Parts Requirements

Every repair requires at minimum a **Junk Die** (d6 Resource Die representing
scavenged or on-hand parts). Roll the Junk Die after completing repairs:

- **3+:** Parts hold; no step-down.
- **1–2:** Parts ran short; step the Junk Die down one.
- **Junk Die Exhausted:** No spare parts of that type remain. Acquire more (see
  <<component-sourcing>>).

| Parts Category | Resource Die | Used For

| Junk / Scrap           | d6  | Field repairs, improvised items, basic mechanical fixes
| Hardware Components    | d8  | Electronics, communications gear, vehicle parts
| Medical Supplies       | d10 | See Resource Dice table in Consumables section
| Specialized Parts      | d6  | High-tech, rare, or artifact-adjacent repairs only — not scavengeable
| Covenant Cache Parts   | d12 | Covenant-supplied; best quality; no acquisition roll needed
|===

[[component-sourcing]]
=== Component Sourcing

Not everything is sitting in a Covenant armory. Field agents must scavenge,
requisition, or improvise.

==== Scavenging

When in a setting that plausibly contains usable materials (hardware store,
junkyard, abandoned building, mechanic's garage), an agent may spend a
**Slow Action** or **Free Move** to scavenge. Roll **Investigate** (WIT), Diff 1.

- **Success:** Find d6 uses worth of Junk/Scrap (or roll on Scavenge table below).
- **Extra Successes (2+):** Upgrade the find to Hardware Components instead of Junk.

[%header,cols="1,3"]
|===
| Scavenge Location | Typical Find (d6)

| Hardware / Home Improvement Store | 1–3: Junk; 4–5: Hardware Components; 6: Power Tools (counts as Specialized Parts)
| Auto Repair Shop / Junkyard       | 1–2: Junk; 3–5: Hardware Components; 6: Vehicle-rated Specialized Parts
| Office Building / University      | 1–3: Junk; 4–5: Electronics (Hardware Components); 6: Lab-grade Specialized Parts
| Abandoned Farmhouse / Rural Site  | 1–4: Junk; 5: Hardware Components; 6: Firearms Parts (Hardware Components)
| Government / Military Facility    | 1: Junk; 2–4: Hardware Components; 5–6: Specialized Parts
| Active Household                  | 1–5: Junk only; 6: Hardware Components (medical kit, power tools)
|===

==== HQ Requisition

Any non-scavengeable part (Specialized Parts, Covenant Cache Parts) requires a
Covenant requisition. Use the standard CL system (see <<requisitioning-gear>>).
Specialized Parts have CL 3. Covenant Cache Parts are CL 4+ and require approval
from the Keep.

**Stack Advantage:** Stack Division agents treat Specialized Parts as CL 2 when
requisitioning (their workshop connections reduce red tape).

[[improvised-items]]
=== Improvised Items

When time is short and a proper tool isn't available, agents can construct
improvised weapons and tools from scavenged materials. Improvised items function
at reduced effectiveness and carry a higher risk of failure.

==== Crafting Improvised Items

Roll **Tech** (WIT), using the difficulty from the table below. Requires at
minimum a **Junk Die (d6)** as parts; step it down after crafting.

Improvised items begin with *Gear Bonus 1* (one black d6 in the pool — never
more, they're held together with duct tape and prayer).

[%header,cols="2,1,1,4"]
|===
| Improvised Item     | Tech Diff | Time | Notes

| *Club / Makeshift Bludgeon* | 1 | 1 minute | Damage 1, Slow. No Gear Die — it's a pipe.
| *Shiv / Field Knife*        | 1 | 5 minutes | Damage 1, Fast. Gear Bonus 1. Breaks on 1 (failure table).
| *Molotov Cocktail*          | 1 | 10 minutes | Blast 1, Fire. No Gear Bonus — single use.
| *Improvised First Aid Kit*  | 2 | 20 minutes | Functions as Basic First Aid. Gear Bonus 1.
| *Signal Jammer*             | 2 | 1 hour | Blocks comms in zone. Gear Bonus 1. Electronics.
| *Tripwire / Alarm Trap*     | 2 | 30 minutes | Spend; triggers on intruder entry. Single use.
| *Garrote Wire*              | 1 | 5 minutes | Grapple-linked attack. No Gear Bonus — simple tool.
| *Lockpick Set (crude)*      | 3 | 1 hour | Deft Hands −1 die compared to proper picks. Gear Bonus 1.
| *Improvised Bug Detector*   | 3 | 2 hours | Detect surveillance; −1 die vs. professional gear. Gear Bonus 1.
| *Field Radio Booster*       | 3 | 2 hours | Extend comms range by 1 zone. Gear Bonus 1. Electronics.
|===

> Improvised items **cannot** be upgraded with proper parts after the fact —
> once improvised, always improvised. The exception is Stack Division agents
> with the *Prototyper* talent, who may grant any improvised item an *Artifact Die (d8)* for a single skill roll — after that roll, the item is permanently destroyed (consistent with the Prototyper talent in xref:chapters/09-divisions.adoc#chapter-divisions[Ch 9 — Divisions]).

==== Improvised Item Failure

When a Gear Die on an improvised item comes up **1**, it does not simply reduce Gear Bonus —
the item fails outright. Roll on the following table:

[%header,cols="1,3"]
|===
| d6 | Improvised Item Failure

| 1 | *Catastrophic Break.* Item is destroyed instantly. If a weapon, the wielder takes 1 Strength damage (no roll to avoid).
| 2 | *Jams or Jams Shut.* Item is unusable this scene. Can be cleared with a free action next round (no roll needed).
| 3 | *Splinters / Shrapnel.* Item breaks; nearest ally in zone rolls *Endure (AGI) Difficulty 1* or takes 1 Strength damage.
| 4 | *Power Surge.* Electronic items only: short circuit, sparks. Make a *Tech (WIT) Difficulty 1* roll; on success, cleared with a Slow Action. On failure, the item is Broken (requires Full Repair).
| 5 | *Reduced Function.* Item still usable but provides no Gear Bonus die (treat as an improvised item with no dice contribution).
| 6 | *Quick Fix.* Near-miss — item is stable. Gear Die stays at current step; no degradation.
|===

=== Stack Division: Crafting Signature

Stack agents aren't just better at repairs — they have a different relationship
with gear entirely. Training in Covenant workshops, maintaining equipment under
operational conditions, and sourcing parts through a network of trusted contacts
gives Stack agents mechanical advantages no other Division matches.

[NOTE]
====
These are *background advantages* intrinsic to Stack training — always active, no activation cost. They do not replace or conflict with Stack's *activated talents* (Jury-Rig, Prototyper, Duct Tape & WD-40, etc.) from xref:chapters/09-divisions.adoc#chapter-divisions[Ch 9]. Both apply independently.
====

[%header,cols="2,4"]
|===
| Advantage | Effect

| *Tech Bonus*          | Stack agents add **+1 die** to all Tech rolls made for repair and improvised item creation.
| *Faster Repairs*      | Quick Repairs take 5 minutes instead of 15. Full Repairs take 2 hours instead of a full Shift.
| *Parts Economy*       | Stack agents never step down a Junk Die on a successful repair — only failed repairs consume parts.
| *Known Contacts*      | Once per case, a Stack agent can source **Hardware Components (d8)** without a Covenant requisition (a contact comes through).
| *Workshop Access*     | At a cell location, Stack agents may use the <<facility-covenant-armorer,Covenant Armorer>> capability at no additional cost, even before it is fully established. They improvise the bench themselves.
|===

[NOTE]
====
*Default crafting rule:* All non-vehicle repairs use a single *Parts Die (d8)* as the parts tracker. Roll it like any Resource Die after each repair — 1–2: step down; 3+: no change. Replenish it at a cell location or during Equipping Phase. This keeps crafting simple and fast at the table.

*Detailed crafting option (DA guidance):* For groups that want more granular resource tracking, the full parts economy (Junk Die, Hardware Components, Specialized Parts, Covenant Cache Parts) described earlier in this section provides deeper material management. Use it when your table enjoys logistical gameplay — but the Parts Die is the default rule.
====
